# Yoruba & Nigerian Heritage — Practical Resources

*The evidence (see `scholars/26-ethnic-racial-socialization.md`) makes this PROTECTIVE for the girls' wellbeing and achievement, not a nice-to-have. This is the practical resource list to act on it.*

## Why native audio matters
Yoruba is tonal — it has three contrastive tones (high, mid, low). The same syllable means different things at different pitches, so **native-audio resources are essential**; text alone teaches the wrong pronunciation.

## Language resources for diaspora children (UK)
- **CultureTree** — children's Yoruba via cartoons, stories and nursery rhymes (UK-based). Strong for young children like Emma.
- **Èdè Yorùbá** — child-focused audio flashcards that address tone (UK-founded).
- **Yorùbá Basics** — online school with live weekend classes (curriculum-based); better for Elsie.
- **Genii Games — Yoruba101 app** — interactive app covering language and culture.

## Culture, history & community (UK)
- **Isese Festival (UK)** — annual celebration of Yoruba heritage.
- **Africa Centre (London)** — events and cultural programming.
- **British Museum** — Ife / Yoruba collections for in-person heritage learning.

## Practical rhythm (tie to the daily plan)
- **Emma:** daily immersion — songs, stories, simple words, pride and belonging from the earliest age.
- **Elsie:** structured lessons (Yorùbá Basics), Nigerian geography/history/peoples, and family lineage — where we come from and what we carry forward.
- **Both:** respect for elders as a taught, practised value; family story as living heritage.

## Verify before committing
Several of these are commercial/advocacy sources, not peer-reviewed. Check current offerings, age-fit and cost directly before subscribing.
