# Acceleration vs Hothousing

**Domain:** Learning Science & Acceleration  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1987-present

## Core idea
Accelerating a ready, willing child is safe and effective; pushing knowledge earlier than developmentally typical (hothousing) is the documented harm.

## What they teach
Acceleration for able, motivated children shows no social-emotional harm across decades of study. The opposite - high-control, pressured, premature instruction - is linked to anxiety, lost motivation and perfectionism.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Let a child who is cruising and eager accelerate in her strong subjects. This is the safe route to 'two years ahead'.

## What they would caution against
THE key caution of the whole programme: forcing pace to hit a benchmark, especially with a young child, is hothousing. Two-years-ahead as a target for pace and provision - never as pressure on the child.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG on both sides: acceleration benefits are robust (Vanderbilt SMPY, 35-yr); hothousing risks are well described. This lens is the guardrail on the 'get ahead' goal.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Accelerate where she is ready and willing; watch for eagerness as the green light.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Highest hothousing risk at age 4 - keep her advancement play-based and pressure-free; ambitious only in concrete skills she takes to naturally.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Is this acceleration (child-led, eager) or hothousing (adult-pushed, pressured)?
- What are the warning signs we would act on to ease off?
- Are we forcing pace to hit the 2027 target rather than following readiness?
