# Lev Vygotsky

**Domain:** Child Development  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1896-1934

## Core idea
Children learn most in the gap between what they can do alone and what they can do with skilled help (the ZPD).

## What they teach
Teach just above independent level with support (scaffolding), via a More Knowledgeable Other, then progressively withdraw support as competence grows.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
The single most practical idea for a tutoring parent. Pitch each task just beyond independent reach, support it, then fade the support.

## What they would caution against
Support that never fades creates dependence; too-hard-without-support creates failure. Calibrate the gap and the fade.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG / foundational and directly actionable in one-to-one teaching.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Find her edge, scaffold the next step, then deliberately withdraw help so she owns it.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Gentle scaffolding in play; do the next-hardest bit with her, then let her try alone.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Is this task in her ZPD, or too easy / too hard?
- Where are we scaffolding but never fading the support?

- Vygotsky, Mind in Society (1978)
