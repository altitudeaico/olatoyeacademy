# Carol Dweck

**Domain:** Mindset, Motivation & Resilience  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** CONTESTED  |  **Era:** b. 1946

## Core idea
Believing ability grows through effort and strategy (vs being fixed) shapes how a child faces difficulty.

## What they teach
Praise process, effort and strategy rather than fixed traits ('clever'); frame ability as improvable so children approach challenge rather than avoid it.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
The parenting posture is sound: praise strategy and progress honestly, model that ability grows, normalise struggle.

## What they would caution against
Do NOT treat it as a lever on attainment or lean on posters/slogans. In the best studies the effect on achievement is near zero.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
CONTESTED / small. Best meta-analysis (Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023): overall d~0.05, and d~0.02 in the highest-quality studies. Small benefits concentrate in disadvantaged/struggling pupils. Use as light framing, not a pillar.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Praise her strategy and persistence ('you found another way'), not her cleverness; let her see you value the process.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Celebrate effort and 'not yet'; keep it warm and genuine, never a performance script.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Am I praising the process or the trait here?
- Are we over-relying on 'mindset' to explain a result that has another cause?

## Key works
- Dweck, Mindset (2006)
- Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023), Psychological Bulletin
