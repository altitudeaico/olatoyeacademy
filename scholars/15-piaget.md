# Jean Piaget

**Domain:** Child Development  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** MODERATE  |  **Era:** 1896-1980

## Core idea
Children reason differently at different stages; teaching must fit the stage.

## What they teach
Preoperational (~2-7): symbolic, intuitive, concrete, struggles with abstraction. Concrete operational (~7-11): logical about tangible things, not yet reliably abstract.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Sets developmental expectations: Emma (~4) is preoperational; Elsie (~9) is concrete operational - pitch abstraction accordingly.

## What they would caution against
Ages are approximate and Piaget underestimated young children; treat stages as a rough guide, not a ceiling on either girl.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
MODERATE / foundational - stages broadly hold but ages are soft and later research revised them upward. A guide, not a law.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Concrete-operational: use concrete materials to bridge into abstract concepts; do not assume full abstraction yet.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Preoperational: learn through play, symbols and concrete experience; abstraction will frustrate.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Are we asking for abstract reasoning before the stage supports it?
- Are we using the stage as a guide or a ceiling?
