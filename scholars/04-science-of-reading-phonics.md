# Systematic Phonics (Science of Reading)

**Domain:** Learning Science & Acceleration  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** MODERATE  |  **Era:** 2000-present

## Core idea
Explicitly and systematically teaching letter-sound relationships is the evidence-based route into reading, especially when begun early.

## What they teach
Teach the code systematically (grapheme-phoneme correspondences) rather than guessing from pictures/context; embed it in a language- and book-rich environment.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Systematic phonics for Emma inside a book-rich home; wide reading across genres for Elsie. Pairs naturally with Mason's 'living books'.

## What they would caution against
A credible dissent (Bowers 2020) argues phonics' superiority over other structured methods is overstated - so hold it as the sensible default, not dogma. Phonics alone is not the whole of reading; comprehension and vocabulary matter.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
MODERATE - the UK legal standard since 2007; National Reading Panel found a moderate effect (d~0.41), larger begun early. Superiority over other structured methods is contested.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Past phonics - keep her reading widely and deeply; focus shifts to comprehension, inference and vocabulary.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Systematic synthetic phonics as the spine of early reading, surrounded by being read to from rich books daily.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Is Emma decoding systematically or guessing from context?
- Are we pairing phonics with enough rich read-aloud?
- For Elsie, have we moved focus from decoding to comprehension?
