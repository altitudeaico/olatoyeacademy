# Benjamin Bloom

**Domain:** Learning Science & Acceleration  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1913-1999

## Core idea
One-to-one tutoring with mastery learning can lift a learner dramatically above classroom-taught peers.

## What they teach
Require a student to master each step before moving on; give frequent corrective feedback; expect that most children can reach high standards given time and good instruction.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Our tutoring-led model IS the intervention Bloom championed. He would endorse 'do not advance until it is secure' as the core rule, and frequent low-stakes checks for understanding.

## What they would caution against
He would caution against the inflated '2 sigma' expectation: the exact two-standard-deviation figure does not replicate. Do not promise miracle gains; realistic one-to-one effect is ~0.79 SD.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG in principle. The mastery+feedback mechanism is well supported; the specific 2-sigma number came from two small studies and is not reliable. Best synthesis (VanLehn 2011): human tutoring ~d=0.79, good tutoring software ~d=0.76.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Set mastery gates per topic; she does not move to the next objective until the current one is secure ('can teach it back').

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Keep steps tiny and success frequent; mastery at her stage means a skill is playful and automatic, not tested.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Would Bloom say Elsie has mastered this, or just met it?
- Where in our week is feedback too infrequent for mastery to hold?
- Are we advancing anyone before the previous step is secure?
