# Ethnic-Racial Socialization (ERS)

**Domain:** Cultural & Heritage Identity  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 2006-present

## Core idea
Teaching heritage, pride, history and language strengthens identity, self-esteem and academic outcomes - and buffers discrimination.

## What they teach
Cultural socialization (heritage, pride, history, language) is consistently linked to positive identity and academic adjustment, with effects strongest for African-heritage youth.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
This is the evidence that makes teaching Yoruba and Nigerian heritage PROTECTIVE, not optional - one of the best-evidenced elements of the whole programme.

## What they would caution against
It is a genuine developmental need, not a cultural 'nice-to-have' - underinvesting has a real cost for the girls' wellbeing and achievement.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG - large, robust literature; a positive ethnic-racial identity buffers the harm of discrimination.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Deliberate, ongoing teaching of Yoruba, Nigerian history and heritage, and family lineage.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Daily heritage immersion - language, stories, customs, pride - from the earliest age.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Are we treating heritage as core curriculum or an add-on?
- Is Yoruba getting genuine daily time, given its protective value?
