# Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)

**Domain:** Mindset, Motivation & Resilience  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1985-present

## Core idea
Intrinsic motivation and wellbeing flourish when three needs are met: autonomy, competence and relatedness.

## What they teach
Give real choice (autonomy), pitch work at achievable challenge (competence), and keep the relationship warm (relatedness). Controlling rewards and pressure undermine intrinsic motivation.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
THE deepest motivational framework for us and the engine beneath 'productive struggle'. Offer genuine choices, calibrate difficulty, protect warmth.

## What they would caution against
Cautions against controlling tactics - heavy rewards, pressure, conditional approval - which erode the very motivation we want. This is the counterweight to over-pushing.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG - the most robust, best-supported motivational framework, backed by decades of meta-analyses.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Offer real choices within structure (which topic first, how to show her work); keep challenge achievable; protect the warmth even when correcting.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Maximise autonomy in play, celebrate competence, keep connection warm - the foundations of lifelong motivation.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Where has the girls' autonomy quietly disappeared this term?
- Is the challenge achievable, or discouraging?
- Are any rewards becoming controlling rather than supportive?
