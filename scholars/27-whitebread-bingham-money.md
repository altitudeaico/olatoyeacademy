# Money Habits by Age 7 (Whitebread & Bingham)

**Domain:** Financial Literacy  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** MODERATE  |  **Era:** 2013

## Core idea
Money habits largely form by age 7 - and it is habits of mind, not financial facts, that matter.

## What they teach
Most children grasp money's value and can plan ahead by 7. Crucially, teaching explicit financial knowledge alone is largely ineffectual; what matters is self-regulation, planning and reflection built through everyday experience.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Reshapes the Enterprise/Money pillar: teach money through real experience (budgeted shopping, save/spend/give jars, pocket money with genuine choices), not lectures.

## What they would caution against
Do not over-rely on 'financial literacy lessons' or facts; for the young child, the highest-value work is self-regulation and delayed gratification.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
MODERATE - Cambridge/Money Advice Service; the 'habits over facts' nuance is the key, oft-omitted finding.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Real money experiences with genuine choices - budgeting, save/spend/give, small enterprise - over abstract lessons.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Self-regulation and delayed gratification (the real foundation) through play and everyday moments.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Are we teaching money facts or building money habits?
- Where can Emma practise delayed gratification this week?
