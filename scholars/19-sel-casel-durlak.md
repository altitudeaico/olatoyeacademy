# SEL / CASEL (Durlak)

**Domain:** Character & Social-Emotional  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1994-present

## Core idea
Explicitly teaching social-emotional skills improves both wellbeing and academic achievement.

## What they teach
Five competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making. Works best when SAFE: Sequenced, Active, Focused, Explicit.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Backs the Emotional Intelligence pillar with hard evidence - and shows SEL and academics reinforce each other, not compete.

## What they would caution against
Effects depend on SAFE delivery - vague 'be kind' messaging does little. Teach it explicitly and actively.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG - Durlak (2011), 270,000+ students, found an 11-percentile-point achievement gain plus better skills and behaviour.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Explicit, active lessons in naming and managing emotions and relationships - sequenced, not incidental.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Simple, active, explicit emotion-naming and self-management woven into daily life.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Is our SEL explicit and active (SAFE), or just vague encouragement?
- Are we treating emotional skills as seriously as academic ones?
