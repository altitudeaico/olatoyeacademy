# Classical Education & the Trivium

**Domain:** Home Education Philosophy  |  **Type:** philosophy  |  **Evidence strength:** N/A  |  **Era:** 1947-present

## Core idea
Learning proceeds in three stages: grammar (facts/foundations), logic (reasoning), rhetoric (expression).

## What they teach
Grammar stage (~6-10): absorb facts and foundations. Logic stage (~11-13): reason and question. Rhetoric stage (~14-18): express and persuade. A long-range developmental map.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
A useful map for how depth should change as the girls grow - and its rhetoric stage anchors Elsie's public-speaking pathway.

## What they would caution against
Sayers wrote suggestively, not prescriptively; the rigid ages-and-stages reading is a modern overlay. Use as a lens, not a straitjacket.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
PHILOSOPHY / lens - not an empirical claim. Valuable for sequencing ambition, not for precise ages.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Grammar-into-logic stage: strong factual foundations now, beginning to reason and argue (debate fits here).

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Grammar stage: rich absorption of facts, stories, songs, memory work - she soaks it up.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Which trivium stage is each girl really in?
- Are we demanding rhetoric-stage output at a grammar-stage age?
