# Attachment & Executive Function

**Domain:** Child Development  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1950s-present

## Core idea
A secure relationship is the base from which children explore and learn; executive function (self-control, working memory, flexibility) is trainable.

## What they teach
Secure attachment enables exploration and risk-taking in learning. Executive functions develop across childhood and improve through play, physical activity, music and self-regulation routines.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Leverage the close home-ed relationship as a natural secure base. Build executive function through movement, music and routines - not worksheets.

## What they would caution against
Stress and harsh pressure degrade executive function and undermine the secure base - the opposite of what we want.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG - both attachment and EF development are well established; EF is trainable via the very activities in our pillars.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Use the secure relationship to let her take on hard challenges; build EF through martial arts, music, planning routines.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Secure warmth first; build EF through play, movement and simple self-regulation games.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Is our pressure strengthening or eroding the secure base?
- Which pillar activity is actually building executive function?
