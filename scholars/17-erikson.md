# Erik Erikson

**Domain:** Child Development  |  **Type:** framework  |  **Evidence strength:** MODERATE  |  **Era:** 1902-1994

## Core idea
Each life stage has a psychosocial task; ages 4 and 9 have specific, different needs.

## What they teach
Age ~4 (Initiative vs Guilt): needs to initiate and lead play without excessive criticism. Age ~9 (Industry vs Inferiority): needs to feel competent - success at real tasks builds worth; harsh comparison breeds inferiority.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
A direct developmental warning: Elsie's stage means competence builds her self-worth - and pressure/comparison can breed inferiority.

## What they would caution against
For Elsie especially: harsh comparison or making her feel inadequate strikes exactly at her stage's vulnerability. For Emma: over-criticising her initiatives induces guilt.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
MODERATE / framework - influential and useful as a lens, not a precise measure.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Give real tasks she can succeed at; celebrate competence; avoid harsh comparison.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Let her initiate and lead play; guide without crushing her initiative.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Are we building Elsie's sense of industry or risking inferiority?
- Are we letting Emma initiate, or over-correcting her?

## Key works
- Erikson, Childhood and Society (1950)
