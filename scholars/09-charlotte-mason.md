# Charlotte Mason

**Domain:** Home Education Philosophy  |  **Type:** philosophy  |  **Evidence strength:** N/A  |  **Era:** 1842-1923

## Core idea
Education is an atmosphere, a discipline, a life; the child is a 'born person' to be fed rich ideas, not dry facts.

## What they teach
Living books over textbooks; short lessons to build the habit of attention; narration (child retells what she learned); deliberate habit training; nature study.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Deeply compatible with modern evidence: her narration is retrieval practice, her short lessons match young attention spans, her habit training is self-regulation. A backbone philosophy for us.

## What they would caution against
Her method assumes unhurried time and rich reading; it resists cramming and over-scheduling - a useful check on our intensity.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
PHILOSOPHY, but unusually well-aligned with replicated science (narration, short lessons, habits). Public domain - full texts freely available.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Narration after readings; keep lessons short and focused; feed her genuinely good books.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Nature study, being read to from living books, gentle habit formation; no formal cramming.

- Would Mason call this a 'living book' or a dry one?
- Are our lessons short enough to protect attention?
- Which habit are we actually training this term?
