# Anders Ericsson

**Domain:** Learning Science & Acceleration  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1947-2020

## Core idea
Expertise comes from deliberate practice - focused, feedback-rich effort at the edge of ability - not from clocked hours.

## What they teach
Practice must target a specific weakness, stretch just beyond current ability, include immediate feedback and correction, and be fully attentive. Volume without these qualities does little.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
He would endorse pointing every 'rep' at a named weakness and building in correction. Quality and design of practice over raw quantity.

## What they would caution against
He would reject the '10,000 hours before 16' framing outright - he said there was never a 10,000-hour rule. Hours are not the target; deliberate design is.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG for deliberate practice as a driver of skill; but it explains only ~18-26% of performance variance (Macnamara 2014), so it is necessary, not sufficient. The '10,000 hours rule' is a MYTHICAL popularisation (Gladwell), not Ericsson's claim.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Each practice block names its target ('today: long division carrying errors'), with immediate correction - not 'do 30 sums'.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Short, focused, feedback-rich bursts; stop while she is still succeeding, not when hours are logged.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Is this practice aimed at a specific weakness or just filling time?
- Where are we counting hours instead of designing practice?
- Does she get immediate feedback in this activity?
