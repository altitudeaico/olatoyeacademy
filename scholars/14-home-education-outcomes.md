# Home Education Outcomes Evidence

**Domain:** Home Education Philosophy  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** WEAK  |  **Era:** 2004-present

## Core idea
Home-educated children often attain at or above average - but the evidence cannot prove home-ed causes it, and structure is the key variable.

## What they teach
Structured home education outperforms both schooling and unstructured 'unschooling' in the best controlled study; unschooled children scored below grade level.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Validates our structured approach specifically. Structure - not merely being home - is what the evidence favours.

## What they would caution against
Be honest: the literature is dominated by self-selected samples confounding socio-economic advantage with any home-ed effect. Do not over-claim causation. There is no UK national data on home-ed GCSE/university outcomes.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
WEAK BY DESIGN - encouraging associations, but self-selected samples. The one robust signal: STRUCTURED beats unstructured (Martin-Chang 2011).

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Keep it structured and evidence-tracked - that is the version with the best support.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Structure from the start; avoid drift into unschooling.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Are we drifting toward unstructured unschooling (the weaker route)?
- Are we honest that our confidence rests on structure, not just being home?
