# John Gottman (Emotion Coaching)

**Domain:** Character & Social-Emotional  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** b. 1942

## Core idea
Treating a child's emotion as a chance to connect and teach builds emotional intelligence.

## What they teach
Five steps: notice the emotion; see it as a chance to connect; listen and validate; help name the feeling; then set limits and problem-solve together.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
A concrete, do-it-today method for the Emotional Intelligence pillar; validated in UK settings; fits both girls.

## What they would caution against
Validation is not permissiveness - step five still sets limits. Skipping straight to limits (or to fixing) misses the connection that makes it work.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG / well-supported and practically trialled in UK schools.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Name and validate the feeling before problem-solving; coach her through big emotions rather than dismissing them.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Notice, name and validate her feelings; then gently set limits - the daily rhythm of emotion coaching.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Did we validate before setting the limit, or skip to correction?
- Are big emotions treated as problems to shut down or chances to coach?

- Gottman & DeClaire, Raising an Emotionally Intelligent Child (1997)
