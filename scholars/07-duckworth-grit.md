# Angela Duckworth

**Domain:** Mindset, Motivation & Resilience  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** CONTESTED  |  **Era:** b. 1970

## Core idea
Grit - passion plus perseverance for long-term goals - is proposed as a driver of achievement.

## What they teach
Sustained effort toward a valued long-term goal, staying with difficulty long enough to improve.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
This is the honest, evidence-based version of the 'winning mindset': train follow-through and perseverance of effort as ordinary conscientiousness.

## What they would caution against
Do not treat 'grit' as a special new trait or magic ingredient. The critique (Crede 2017) shows it is largely redundant with conscientiousness, and only 'perseverance of effort' carries weight - not 'consistency of interests'.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
CONTESTED. Crede et al. (2017), 'Much Ado About Grit': grit correlates only moderately with performance and very strongly with conscientiousness (r~.72). Real but oversold.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Build follow-through: finish what she starts, stay with a hard task past the frustration point, with warmth not harshness.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Too young for grit framing; build persistence through play she enjoys.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Is this 'grit' or just ordinary conscientiousness we can build directly?
- Are we confusing perseverance with never quitting anything?
