# Retrieval Practice & Spacing

**Domain:** Learning Science & Acceleration  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** STRONG  |  **Era:** 1885-present

## Core idea
Recalling information (self-testing) and spacing it over time produces far stronger long-term memory than rereading.

## What they teach
Retrieval beats review: pulling knowledge from memory strengthens it. Spacing beats cramming. Interleaving mixed problem types beats blocking one type. Rereading feels productive but creates an illusion of competence.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Daily low-stakes recall (narration, flashcards, quick quizzing) and deliberately revisiting old material on a schedule. Charlotte Mason's 'narration' is retrieval practice a century early.

## What they would caution against
Cautions against passive review (rereading, highlighting) as the main study method - it feels effective but is weak. Do not mistake fluency-while-looking for knowing.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
STRONG - among the most replicated findings in all of learning science across ages and subjects.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
End each topic with 'tell me what you learned' (narration) and revisit last week's material in a short Monday recall.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Playful recall - 'what happened in the story?', naming games - spaced across days, not one sitting.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Where are we rereading when we should be recalling?
- What old material is due for a spaced revisit this week?
- Could we interleave these problem types instead of blocking them?
