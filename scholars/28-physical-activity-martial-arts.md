# Physical Activity, Cognition & Martial Arts

**Domain:** Physical Development  |  **Type:** empirical  |  **Evidence strength:** MODERATE  |  **Era:** 2004-present

## Core idea
Physical activity supports cognition via executive function; martial arts may specifically build self-regulation and discipline.

## What they teach
Exercise improves executive function, attention and (indirectly) academic performance. Martial arts training has been linked to better self-regulation, conduct and executive functioning than standard PE.

## What they would endorse for Olatoye Academy
Backs the Physical/Martial pillar: martial arts is a credible, discipline- and focus-building choice with plausible self-regulation benefits - and a healthy arena for learning to lose and recover.

## What they would caution against
Martial-arts samples are modest and mostly non-randomised - hold the specific numbers lightly. The benefit runs through executive function, not straight to grades.

## Evidence strength (read honestly)
MODERATE - exercise-cognition link is solid; martial-arts-specific claims are promising but based on small studies.

## For Elsie (Year 5 / KS2)
Martial arts for discipline, resilience and self-regulation; a safe place to lose, recover and try again.

## For Emma (Reception / EYFS)
Lots of movement and active play (the EF foundation); martial arts when developmentally ready.

## Pressure-test prompts
- Are we choosing martial arts for self-regulation, holding the numbers lightly?
- Is there enough daily movement to support executive function?
